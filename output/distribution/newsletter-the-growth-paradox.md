Hey,

I need to confess something.

I've been that person. The one with the morning routine that takes longer than my actual morning. The meditation streak that I'd feel guilty about breaking. The journaling habit that turned my inner life into a quarterly earnings report.

And you know what? I was more anxious, more brittle, and less actually alive than I'd been in years.

This week's article hit me hard because it names something I've suspected for a long time but was too afraid to admit: the self-improvement industry is actively making us worse.

---

**Key insight from this week:**

Growth is a byproduct, not a goal. A study found that people who took deliberate "non-practice" breaks showed 40% faster skill consolidation than those who practiced continuously. Not 4%. Forty percent. The brain doesn't integrate learning during practice — it integrates during rest. The default mode network, the brain's daydreaming circuit, lights up when you're not focused on anything. That's when the system reorganizes. That's when the connections form.

---

I've been thinking about this in the context of my own life. As a doctor, I'm trained to optimize. As a coder, I'm trained to debug. As a human being who's been through some things, I'm trained to "work on myself."

But here's what I'm starting to realize: the version of me that's trying so hard to grow is the version that's stuck. Every morning I wake up and do my routine, I'm subtly confirming that the raw, unoptimized version of me isn't enough. That I'm a problem to be solved.

That's not a growth mindset. That's a shame engine with a tracking app.

So I'm trying something different. I'm deleting the habit apps. I'm taking days where I'm not trying to grow — just living. Not "practicing presence." Just present. Not "working on relationships." Just relating.

It's terrifying. It's also the most alive I've felt in years.
